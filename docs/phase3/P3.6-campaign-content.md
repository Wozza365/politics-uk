### P3.6 — Campaign objectives, scenario arcs, and replayable content `TODO`

**Goal.** Give each campaign a clear dramatic shape and reasons to replay it. P2.6 increases event
volume and P2.10 adds dated scenarios; this task makes content react to the campaign state and
gives players goals beyond passively awaiting the next General Election.

**Depends on.** P2.6 and P2.10. P3.3 provides action records, P3.5 provides resolved election
history, and P3.7 will expose this content clearly. It can begin with state/validation work before
all election tiers are resolved.

**Content principles.**

- Scenarios are curated, validated data—never unreviewed runtime text generation.
- Objectives explain why a player cares, but do not replace the core electoral win condition with
  arbitrary score chasing. They can be primary, optional, hidden-until-triggered, or historical.
- Arcs are short, stateful chains with a beginning, escalation, player choice, and lasting result.
  They use the existing deterministic event/action architecture, not a separate story engine.
- The game must distinguish reported facts, scenario assumptions, and fictional events in UI and
  authoring metadata where material to player understanding.

**Steps:**

1. Extend scenario data with a typed `campaign` section: opening brief, electoral horizon,
   primary objective(s), optional objectives, feature flags/expected tiers, and scenario-specific
   tuning values. Add a versioned JSON schema/type and validator checks; preserve the existing
   2025 scenario as a valid minimal campaign.
2. Define objective state and a pure evaluator. Objectives may observe polling, resources, action
   history, election outcomes, contest outcomes, and dates; they may not mutate the game directly.
   Store lifecycle records (`available`, `active`, `succeeded`, `failed`, `expired`) in save state.
3. Add an arc/event schema extension for prerequisites, stages, branches, cooldown/one-shot rules,
   and named consequences. Validate referential integrity (every action, target, policy, party,
   and follow-up stage exists) at data-build time.
4. Author one complete vertical slice for `uk-2025-01-01`: an opening briefing, two or three
   optional objectives appropriate to different party sizes, and at least two branching arcs whose
   choices exercise party levers, contested elections, or local campaigning. Aim for variety, not
   volume; P2.6 remains the event-library volume task.
5. Add a campaign journal/objectives surface. It shows active goals, completed consequences,
   time limits, and concise links to relevant map areas/events. It must not reveal hidden
   prerequisites or spoil branches before the player can know them.
6. Extend the scenario picker to show scenario briefing, expected duration/horizon, available
   parties, and a plain-language difficulty/objective summary. Keep selecting a scenario separate
   from starting it so a player can read before committing.
7. Test objective evaluation and arc branching as pure fixtures, schema validation failures, save
   restoration part-way through an arc, and an end-to-end authored-scenario smoke path.

**Files:** `src/types/{scenario,event,objective}.ts`, scenario JSON and `sources.json` metadata,
`scripts/data/validate-scenario.mjs`, new `src/sim/{objectives,arcs}.ts`, campaign/journal UI,
and tests.

**Acceptance:** a new campaign begins with a comprehensible brief and at least one active goal;
choices can unlock or close a documented arc; objective outcomes persist through save/load and are
visible in a journal. Invalid content relationships fail validation before reaching players.
