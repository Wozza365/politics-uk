### P4.3 - Map, geography, overlays, and region-detail polish `DONE`

**Goal.** Make the central map feel like the premium core of the game. Geography should have depth,
clarity, responsive interaction, and political meaning without sacrificing the renderer abstraction
or performance on large tiers.

**Depends on.** P4.0 and preferably P4.2 for controls/tooltips.

**Steps:**

1. Establish a cartographic background treatment: subtle map-room texture, water/land separation,
   optional latitude/longitude grid, controlled vignette, and enough negative space for HUD panels.
   Avoid decorative blobs or gradients that do not reinforce the map.
2. Refine region rendering for SVG and hex modes: fill, border, disabled state, hover, selected,
   active zoom, targeted commitment, opponent activity, contest, and player-owned state. Provide
   non-colour cues such as stroke patterns, dashes, hatching, glyphs, or labels for important
   overlays.
3. Improve depth and lighting: raised-edge colour, shadow softness, selected-region lift, and hover
   highlight should feel intentional across Westminster, regional, council, and ward views. Keep
   effects cheap enough for large maps.
4. Redesign map tooltips and active-region detail into a premium information sheet: constituency or
   council name, holder, majority/vote share, demographic notes, contest/targeting state, action
   affordances, and provenance/estimated flags where relevant.
5. Add a compact overlay legend that can explain commitments, contests, opponent activity, disabled
   regions, and selected regions without permanently covering the map. It should collapse cleanly on
   narrow screens.
6. Upgrade zoom/pan controls with icons, stable hit areas, disabled states, keyboard support, and
   optional mini status readout. Manual SVG icons should be replaced by the chosen icon system.
7. Verify the renderer abstraction remains intact: visual polish belongs in renderer options,
   region-state metadata, tokens, or wrapper UI, not in game logic.
8. Profile the largest supported map views after visual effects are added. If shadows, filters, or
   patterns become expensive, add measured fallbacks rather than guessing.

**Files:** `src/components/MapView.vue`, `src/map/SvgMapRenderer.ts`, `src/map/HexMapRenderer.ts`,
`src/map/MapRenderer.ts`, region-state builders, `src/style.css`, map-related tests, and Phase 4
visual QA screenshots.

**Acceptance:** the map is recognisably the centrepiece at first glance. Hover, selection, targeting,
contest, and disabled states are legible without colour alone. SVG and hex renderers both remain
usable, keyboard-accessible alternatives still exist, and large map interactions stay within the
P4.9 performance budget.
